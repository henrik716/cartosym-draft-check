Walkthrough of PR #192: renders in the Part 1 document

Part 1 of OGC Cartographic Symbology 2.0 gets rendered examples on Norwegian data, with CartoSym-CSS and CartoSym-JSON next to each render, and the requirements that independent readers rendered differently get tighter wording. Pull request #192 (branch example-renders); nothing here is an SWG decision until the group makes it. Jérôme's replies on #192 (16–17 November 2026) are shown under each change: he agrees with 8, answered 6 differently, and left 4 open. Since 17 November the branch follows these answers; the changes, and the choices made where an answer left one open, are in the decision log under “Changes after the replies”.

1. The examples

All examples use one area, Rondane in Norway: Kartverket N50 Kartdata (land cover, rivers, roads, cabins, protected areas, place names), the national terrain model and a Sentinel-2 scene, all CC BY 4.0 or Copernicus. In the document each figure sits above a two-column table with the style in CartoSym-CSS and CartoSym-JSON. The JSON is converted from the CSS with pycartosym and validated against the schema of main and of #188; pycartosym 0.6.5 needs one fix-up, renaming centerLine to center (until #188). The elevation model is fetched for each panel at about one cell per screen pixel (2 m at 1:4 000, 50 m for the whole area), and the Sentinel-2 scene of 1 September 2024 is cloud-free. The numbered vector examples are drawn over a pale map (forest, farmland, water, rivers and the grey shadows of the relief, leaving out any layer the example styles itself), with a scale bar and a credit; the caption says these are context, not part of the style. Coverage examples 5 and 7, which are 80 % opaque, show the relief shadows through them. The 21 figures under the requirements are vignettes: rows of small panels of one place that each change one value, with pale lakes as the only context (Jérôme on #185 and #192); the earlier requirement maps are kept as a gallery outside the document. Examples 1 to 4, 10 and 11 were rewritten for Rondane; 2, 3 and 4 also differ from the SWG's originals beyond the data: colours and stroke widths from the shared palette, road classes in 3, a Dot at every scale in 4 (the default white Dot disappeared on the map), and SVG icons in 2 (the draft renderer draws every Image 24 px wide, so 24 px PNGs were blurred in the 2× figures). The structure of each style, and what it shows, is the SWG's. In the N50 data, coordinates are kept to 0.1 m, and road and river pieces that meet end to end with the same properties are joined into one line.

2. How the wording was checked

The question: does an implementation written only from the requirement text draw the same maps as another one? Six fresh Claude sessions (three Opus, two Sonnet, one Haiku) each got the requirement text of main without figures and the render kits of the 20 requirement examples, and wrote a renderer from the text alone, listing every choice the text left open. They could not see the draft renderer, pycartosym, libCartoSym, the web or each other. Where they split, one reading was written into the requirement, preferring in order: what the document shows (its figures and examples), consistency with the rest of the document, the SE 1.1 mapping in Annex B, and what most readers chose. Three fresh runs on the tightened text then checked the result. Three more sessions wrote expression evaluators from the text for the 123 cases of the test harness.

5. Left for issues

  • Schema and text names, all in existing issues: center/centerLine and dashOffset (#120, fixed in #188); dataLayer.identifier/dataLayer.id and visualization.*/viz.* (#168); url/uri, the alignment members and the unit lists (#169); colour ranges 0 to 1 and 0 to 255 (#150).
  • Rendering points no reader could settle from the text (#194). Answered by Jérôme and now in the branch: no miter limit (round joins), nothing for a stroke of width 0, an Image at its own size in pixels, linear light allowed for colour maps and tints, overviews allowed for maps coarser than the coverage, casing and centre line width 0 by default, and hill shading off by default. Still open: how a number is written as text, defaults for fonts and outlines, the unit of a font size without one, and an Image size (#100).
  • Expressions: precedence and associativity exist only in the order of the grammar's alternatives (-2 ^ 2 is 4, 2 ^ 3 ^ 2 is 64): #118, #178, #180. NULL: #155, where a comment points out that the Logical Operators table is still two-valued. Added to #173: random() parses as an empty instance, not as a function call, as arguments needs at least one expression (fixed by #196); true, false and null are not tokens of the lexer and parse as identifiers, which Jérôme answered is intended.
  • pycartosym: 0.6.4 dropped marker: null, dashOffset and dash lengths with units, turned a bare identifier in label placement into a string, and kept a hex colour of a Dot as a string (maxcollombin/pycartosym #174, #182 to #185). 0.6.5 fixes all of these, and converts every example without fix-ups except casing, where it writes centerLine, the name in main’s schema until #188.
